\section{Evaluation Scenarios}
\label{sec:case_study_scenarios}

This section evaluates the controlled-pendulum case study in three scenarios.
Each scenario uses the references and metrics defined in Section~\ref{sec:case_study_reference_metrics}.
The baseline scenario verifies the \ac{FMU}-only closed-loop co-simulation against the monolithic OpenModelica reference.
The contact scenario evaluates the integrated workflow with hybrid events, \ac{PID} reset, and runtime plant-model switching.
The performance scenario benchmarks the computational benefit of activating the \ac{FEM} model only near contact.

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\subsection{Baseline Closed Loop Without Contact}
\label{sec:case_study_baseline}

The baseline scenario is the numerical-verification case for the \ac{FMU}-only closed loop without contact.
The system contains the setpoint generator, \ac{PID} controller, dynamic drive, and \ac{FMU} pendulum plant.
Sensor sampling and quantization are disabled.
This isolates the numerical effect of co-simulation coupling and of the macro communication step size.

The monolithic reference is the OpenModelica model \texttt{NoContact.Baseline}.
The primary comparison variable is the pendulum angle \(\theta\), because it is the closed-loop plant output.
The comparison uses \(E_{\infty,\theta}\) and \(E_{2,\theta}\) as defined in Section~\ref{sec:case_study_reference_metrics}.
The co-simulation is repeated for
\(\Delta t \in \{0.02, 0.01, 0.005, 0.001\}\,\mathrm{s}\).
The expected behavior is a decreasing trajectory error as the macro step size is refined.

\begin{figure}[t]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/6_case_study/controlled_pendulum/validation/baseline_step_convergence.pdf}
    \caption[Baseline closed-loop co-simulation without contact]{Baseline closed-loop co-simulation without contact.
    Panel~(a) compares the full angle trajectory for different macro step sizes with the monolithic OpenModelica reference.
    Panel~(b) magnifies the first \(0.4\,\mathrm{s}\), where the coarse macro step produces the largest visible deviation.
    Panel~(c) reports the sampled angle errors \(E_{\infty,\theta}\) and \(E_{2,\theta}\) against the interpolated reference solution.
    The horizontal axis in panel~(c) is inverted so that macro-step refinement proceeds from left to right.}
    \label{fig:case_baseline_convergence}
\end{figure}

Figure~\ref{fig:case_baseline_convergence} shows the expected refinement behavior.
For \(\Delta t=0.02\,\mathrm{s}\), the angle trajectory visibly deviates from the reference during the first rise and the deviation grows over the full horizon.
For \(\Delta t=0.01\,\mathrm{s}\) and smaller, the trajectory follows the reference much more closely.
The zoomed panel confirms that the remaining differences decrease with the communication step size.

The error plot confirms the visual interpretation.
Both \(E_{\infty,\theta}\) and \(E_{2,\theta}\) decrease monotonically over the tested step sizes.
This verifies the expected convergence behavior of the \ac{FMU}-only closed-loop co-simulation toward the monolithic OpenModelica reference.

\subsection{Multi-Model Switching With FEM Contact}
\label{sec:case_study_multimodel_contact}

The second scenario evaluates the integrated contact workflow.
It reuses the closed-loop system of Section~\ref{sec:case_study_controlled_pendulum_system}.
The plant is represented by the \texttt{MasterPendulum} component.
It exposes the shared plant interface and selects internally between the \ac{FMU} rigid-body model away from contact, the OpenSim model in an intermediate region, and the \ac{FEM} model close to the wall.
A contact event is routed to the active plant model and to the controller.
When the active model is the \ac{FMU} or OpenSim model, the plant-side handler reverses the angular velocity.
When the active model is the \ac{FEM} model, contact is resolved by the compliant contact formulation and no explicit velocity reset is applied.
The controller-side handler resets the \ac{PID} integrator.

The monolithic reference is the OpenModelica model \texttt{Contact.RigidContact} with integrator reset enabled.
The reference uses an ideal rigid impact law.
The \syssimx{} configuration uses a stiff compliant \ac{FEM} contact formulation during contact-relevant phases.
The comparison verifies numerical consistency with the rigid-contact reference and validates the heterogeneous switched workflow.
It does not provide physical validation of the \ac{FEM} contact law.

The simulation horizon is \(t_{\mathrm{end}}=0.4\,\mathrm{s}\), and the macro communication step size is \(\Delta t=10^{-3}\,\mathrm{s}\).
At the \(3\,\mathrm{Hz}\) setpoint frequency, this horizon spans one full setpoint cycle and captures the complete wall-contact and rebound sequence.
The \ac{FEM} pendulum uses Young's modulus \(E=2.1\cdot 10^{11}\,\mathrm{Pa}\), Poisson ratio \(\nu=0.3\), and density \(\rho=7850\,\mathrm{kg\,m^{-3}}\).
The normal contact stiffness is \(k_n=2\cdot 10^9\,\mathrm{N\,m^{-1}}\).
The \texttt{MasterPendulum} starts in \ac{FMU} mode and uses a dwell time of \(0.01\,\mathrm{s}\) for switching hysteresis.

The primary plant-side quantities are the exposed angle \(\theta\) and the contact-event times.
The controller-side quantities are the controller command \(u_{\mathrm{control}}\) and the \ac{PID} integrator state \(I_{\mathrm{out}}\).
The angle error is reported over the full horizon and over the contact window \(t\in[0.15,0.35]\,\mathrm{s}\).
The event-time error is computed from the five paired contact events of the co-simulation and the rigid-contact reference.

Figure~\ref{fig:case_multimodel_contact} shows that the switched trajectory follows the rigid-contact reference during free motion.
The largest deviations occur during the repeated rebounds at the wall.
This behavior follows from the different contact laws.
The reference applies an ideal velocity reflection.
The co-simulation resolves contact through a stiff compliant \ac{FEM} model.

The mode strip records six accepted mode switches.
The first two switches form a short \ac{FMU}--OpenSim--\ac{FMU} start-up transient.
The second OpenSim interval hands over to \ac{FEM} shortly before the contact-relevant phase.
After the repeated contacts, the plant returns through OpenSim to \ac{FMU} mode.

Table~\ref{tab:case_multimodel_metrics} quantifies the trajectory, controller, and event-time deviations.
The maximum angle error is \(5.309\cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{rad}\), which corresponds to approximately \(0.30^\circ\).
This maximum occurs inside the contact window, so the contact-window and full-horizon \(E_{\infty,\theta}\) values coincide.

All five contact events of the reference are reproduced.
The largest event-time error is \(3.729\cdot 10^{-4}\,\mathrm{s}\), which is below one half of the macro communication step.
The \ac{PID} reset event connection fires on each contact event.

The controller-side result shows that the localized contact events also reach the control loop.
Figure~\ref{fig:case_multimodel_controller} shows the controller command and the integrator state.
The controller command follows the reference over the full horizon, including actuator saturation during contact.
The integrator state is reset at each localized contact event.
Its largest pointwise deviation is a short reset-time offset where the co-simulation and the reference differ by one macro step.

The switching consistency is evaluated from the \texttt{sync\_events} log of the \texttt{MasterPendulum}.
Across the six accepted switches, the maximum state jump is \(8.33\cdot 10^{-17}\,\mathrm{rad}\) for \(\theta\) and \(1.24\cdot 10^{-14}\,\mathrm{rad\,s^{-1}}\) for \(\omega\).

This scenario validates the integrated workflow rather than the isolated hybrid algorithm.
The isolated event-detection and event-handling mechanisms were verified in Section~\ref{sec:impl_hybrid}.
The result here combines heterogeneous pendulum models, hybrid contact events, runtime model switching, and \ac{PID} reset in one closed-loop co-simulation.

\subsection{Runtime Performance of Switched FEM Usage}
\label{sec:case_study_performance}

The third scenario benchmarks the computational benefit of runtime model switching.
It uses the same contact mechanics as Section~\ref{sec:case_study_multimodel_contact}.
One configuration keeps the \ac{FEM} pendulum active over the full trajectory.
The second configuration uses the \texttt{MasterPendulum} and activates the \ac{FEM} model only near the wall.
Both configurations use the same macro communication step, \ac{FEM} internal step, material parameters, contact stiffness, and input trajectory.
The simulation horizon is \(t_{\mathrm{end}}=0.4\,\mathrm{s}\).
The macro communication step and the \ac{FEM} internal step are both \(10^{-3}\,\mathrm{s}\).
The contact stiffness is \(k_n=2\cdot 10^9\,\mathrm{N\,m^{-1}}\).
The switched configuration uses the position threshold \(0.075\,\mathrm{rad}\) for \ac{FEM} activation and a dwell time of \(0.05\,\mathrm{s}\).
\ac{FEM} activation is additionally locked out before \(t=0.10\,\mathrm{s}\) so that the controlled-pendulum upswing always runs on the rigid-body \ac{FMU} and the first \ac{FEM} activation coincides with the contact phase.

The full-\ac{FEM} configuration is used as the high-fidelity benchmark for this comparison.
The switched contact workflow was already evaluated against the monolithic OpenModelica reference in Section~\ref{sec:case_study_multimodel_contact}.
This scenario therefore reports a benchmark of computational cost.
It does not establish correctness on its own.

Figure~\ref{fig:case_performance_switching} shows the trajectory and timing comparison.
The mode strip shows that the switched configuration enters \ac{FEM} mode shortly before the first wall contact.
It remains in \ac{FEM} mode throughout the repeated bounce sequence and returns to \ac{FMU} mode afterwards.
The two trajectories agree closely outside the contact window.
The visible differences are concentrated during the rebounds.

Table~\ref{tab:case_performance_metrics} summarizes the runtime and trajectory metrics.
The switched configuration reduces the median total wall time from \(467.6\,\mathrm{s}\) to \(289.6\,\mathrm{s}\).
This corresponds to an end-to-end speedup of \(1.61\times\).
The \ac{FEM} solver wall time decreases from \(458.3\,\mathrm{s}\) to \(222.1\,\mathrm{s}\), which corresponds to a reduction by \(2.06\times\).
This reduction is consistent with the smaller number of \ac{FEM} solver invocations and with the reduced \ac{FEM}-active simulated time.
The switched trajectory differs from the full-\ac{FEM} benchmark by \(E_{\infty,\theta}=2.839\cdot 10^{-2}\,\mathrm{rad}\) over the full horizon.
This value reports the numerical cost of switching relative to the full-\ac{FEM} benchmark.

The benchmark shows whether runtime model switching reduces computational cost for the controlled-pendulum case study.
The expected benefit is obtained by avoiding \ac{FEM} execution during phases where the high-fidelity contact representation is not needed.
The expected cost is a contact-window deviation relative to the full-\ac{FEM} benchmark.
This accuracy and runtime tradeoff is discussed in Section~\ref{sec:case_study_discussion}.
